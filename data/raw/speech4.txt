Innovation often begins with noticing a problem that others have accepted as normal. A student may find a better way to organise study material. A farmer may use information to manage water more carefully. A small business may improve its service by listening to customers.

Not every idea will succeed on the first attempt. Testing an idea on a small scale can reveal what works and what needs to change. Instead of being discouraged by an unsuccessful experiment, we can treat it as useful evidence. Improvement comes from observing, testing and refining.

We should make room for practical creativity in schools, workplaces and communities. When people are encouraged to ask questions and share suggestions, useful solutions can emerge from unexpected places.
